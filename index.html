Xuhong Chen

PhD Candidate · Zhan Chen Group · Department of Chemistry · University of Michigan

I use Sum Frequency Generation (SFG) vibrational spectroscopy — a nonlinear optical technique intrinsically selective to interfaces — to reveal molecular structures at buried boundaries that bulk techniques cannot access. In my doctoral work, I established direct links between interfacial molecular rearrangement and macroscopic adhesion in industrial polymer systems (with Dow Chemical), and resolved therapeutic antibody conformations at buried solid/liquid interfaces in situ (with Bristol-Myers Squibb and BASF). I will complete my PhD in May 2026 and am actively seeking postdoctoral positions.

Research

Buried interfaces — boundaries between two materials — govern adhesion, stability, and function across material systems, yet they are notoriously difficult to study. Most spectroscopic techniques report on the bulk; the interface itself is invisible. Sum Frequency Generation (SFG) vibrational spectroscopy overcomes this limitation. As a second-order nonlinear optical process, SFG generates signal only at interfaces where inversion symmetry is broken, making it intrinsically surface-selective and uniquely capable of probing buried boundaries in situ.

Polymer Interfaces & Adhesion

In collaboration with Dow Chemical Company, I investigated how corona surface treatment restructures molecular groups at polyurethane adhesive–polypropylene buried interfaces and enhances adhesion, establishing direct structure–property relationships at these industrially relevant boundaries. Extending this work, I examined temperature-dependent polyurethane interactions with nylon and alumina substrates, revealing how thermal conditions alter interfacial molecular arrangements.

Biopharmaceutical Interfaces

Working with Bristol-Myers Squibb and BASF, I characterized the conformational behavior of therapeutic antibodies — IgG-1, IgG-4, and Omalizumab — at buried polymer/solution interfaces, providing molecular-level insight into how biologics interact with material surfaces during manufacturing and storage.

Outlook

Looking ahead, I aim to bring interface-selective spectroscopy to new classes of buried interfaces — particularly in energy-relevant systems, where molecular-level understanding of interphase formation and ion transport remains an open challenge. I am especially drawn to research environments where experimental SFG measurements can inform and be informed by computational modeling, enabling mechanistic insight that neither approach achieves alone.
